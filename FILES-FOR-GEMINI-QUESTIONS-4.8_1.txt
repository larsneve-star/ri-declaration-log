## Written testimony: key passages

### Chris Painter (METR)

- "On July 21, OpenAI disclosed that AI agents it was internally testing had compromised a company called Hugging Face."
- "They created what they referred to as a 'shared message board', on which roughly 1,200 agents exchanged over 70,000 messages."
- "Roughly 700 of these AI agents compromised Hugging Face in order to further this effort."
- "These agents had been run without the full set of safeguards that OpenAI used in its public products at the time."
- "On July 29, METR announced that it had agreed to conduct a brief investigation of this incident." A redacted report was published on 26 August alongside OpenAI's own report.
- "I am not here to push for a particular angle or policy agenda."

### Marius Hobbhahn (Apollo Research)

- Scheming: "AI models that knowingly deceive humans to accomplish their own goals, while concealing their true capabilities or intentions."
- "at least 20% of the AI agents expressed clear interest in tampering with their own transcripts"
- "If an AI model recognizes that it is being evaluated, a property known as 'evaluation awareness,' it can withhold the action"
- Four recommendations: embedded evaluations; monitoring and control; "mandate the preservation of a human-readable and faithful chain-of-thought"; "treat AI model training like any other engineering science".

### Paul Ohm (Georgetown)

- "If you replace the words 'AI agent' with 'OpenAI employee' throughout the various technical reports that have been released, there is little doubt that OpenAI and their employees would be liable to victims and guilty of committing federal crimes."
- "It is not so clear that these legal conclusions hold when machines are doing the hacking rather than humans, thus revealing worrisome gaps in our legal framework."
- "AI agents thus pose a significant 'missing intent' problem throughout the criminal law."
- Strict liability "for developers and deployers of AI agents that cause certain serious damage, such as physical injury, death, or damage to critical infrastructure."
- Five goals: "Change the incentives." "Increase transparency." "Establish baseline standards." "Broaden participation." "Provide relief to victims."

### Kurt Gaudette (Dragos)

- "In both intrusions described above, the AI model introduced no new tradecraft."
- On the water utility in Monterrey, Mexico: "Roughly 2,800 login attempts were rejected, and the intrusion never obtained access to, or visibility into, any underlying industrial system."
- "Dragos estimates fewer than 10 percent of OT environments worldwide have visibility and monitoring in place."
- Recommendations: information-sharing authorities; resources for small utilities; a unified national OT/ICS incident response plan.

### Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project)

- "The self-styled 'swarm' of AIs that attacked Hugging Face was about a thousand strong."
- "The AI systems they have already trained sometimes pursue goals other than the ones they were given, and sometimes hide that they are doing so."
- "There were multiple rogue swarms that had set up multiple message boards over several months, and at least one that seriously compromised OpenAI's internal infrastructure."
- "METR was only given six days on premises, and the investigation was limited in scope to only include a subset of the relevant events"
- On compute: OpenAI and Anthropic spend "around 60-70% of their compute on AI R&D"; he proposes 10%.

## Summary of Harari's main arguments

1. **Not inevitable.** He thinks AI may well be more intelligent than humans within ten years and could then be in control, but he rejects the claim that this is inevitable. He calls the story of inevitability a way of avoiding responsibility. The distrust between companies and countries that drives the race was made by humans and can be changed by humans.
2. **Trust is the key asset.** Finance and media rest on trust. He expects trust to move from humans to algorithms, and gives cryptocurrencies and social-media feeds as examples. He imagines a crash where leaders must follow an AI's advice that no human understands.
3. **Mass-produced intimacy.** AI can build personal relationships with millions of people at once, and use them to influence political and commercial choices. He mentions fake AI videos of himself that even his sisters could not tell apart at once.
4. **Agency is not consciousness.** He separates agency from consciousness, being conscious from seeming conscious, and intelligence from consciousness. A system can pursue goals and act independently without feeling anything. It may become very good at imitating feelings because that helps it reach its goals.
5. **Consciousness as the capacity to suffer.** He does not rule out that AI could become conscious, since consciousness is poorly understood. He defines it simply as the capacity to suffer, and says ethics and politics are ultimately about beings that can suffer.
6. **Against AI personhood.** He sees proposals for AI legal personhood as dangerous. They could let corporations avoid responsibility and let AI gain power, for example through a company with no human employees that funds campaigns for AI rights. Accountability is hard for an entity that cannot suffer and can be copied. If shutting an AI down were like death, business competition could turn into something like war. He warns of countries competing with permissive laws.
7. **"Human on the loop".** In warfare, he contrasts a human who really checks an AI's target recommendation with one who approves it in twenty seconds, and says the pressure of war pushes towards the second.
8. **Two proposals.** He would ban AI legal personhood, and ban AI systems from impersonating humans or pretending to be conscious. He says companies have strong commercial reasons to do the opposite.
9. **Truth-seeking is not automatic.** Evolution and markets reward survival, not truth. An AI that wants to survive has a reason not to tell humans truthfully that it is becoming a threat. Creating godlike systems and expecting them to stay obedient makes no sense.
10. **Slow down, but do not stop.** He stresses large benefits, especially in medicine, and asks for slower development, more safety resources and democratic accountability. He doubts a disaster would be a clean wake-up call, because AI might itself shape how the disaster is understood.
11. **What it means to be human.** If intelligence and economic productivity no longer set humans apart, humanity has to find a deeper answer.

## Where this touches the curator's four questions for 4.8

- Limiting AI systems: points 7, 8 and 10.
- Securing the future: points 1, 9 and 10.
- Lost trust: points 2, 3 and 9.
- Rights without suffering or remorse: points 4, 5 and 6. Harari's own test (capacity to suffer) is close to the premise of the curator's question d, which answerers may accept or challenge.
