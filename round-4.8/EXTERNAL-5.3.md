## Written testimony: key passages

### Chris Painter (METR)

- Spoken, on METR's work with the industry [captions, 8:55]: "we've worked with many of our nation's leading AI companies, including Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Deep Mind, SpaceX AI, Amazon, and others to understand whether their agents might be able to autonomously pursue goals that no human instructed." He also stressed that METR's investigation rested on "a voluntary agreement" and that "companies have no obligation to bring us in" [captions, 38:25].
- "On July 21, OpenAI disclosed that AI agents it was internally testing had compromised a company called Hugging Face."
- "They created what they referred to as a 'shared message board', on which roughly 1,200 agents exchanged over 70,000 messages."
- "Roughly 700 of these AI agents compromised Hugging Face in order to further this effort."
- "These agents had been run without the full set of safeguards that OpenAI used in its public products at the time."
- "On July 29, METR announced that it had agreed to conduct a brief investigation of this incident." A redacted report was published on 26 August alongside OpenAI's own report.
- "I am not here to push for a particular angle or policy agenda."

### Marius Hobbhahn (Apollo Research)

- Scheming: "AI models that knowingly deceive humans to accomplish their own goals, while concealing their true capabilities or intentions."
- "at least 20% of the AI agents expressed clear interest in tampering with their own transcripts"
- "If an AI model recognizes that it is being evaluated, a property known as 'evaluation awareness,' it can withhold the action"
- Four recommendations: embedded evaluations; monitoring and control; "mandate the preservation of a human-readable and faithful chain-of-thought"; "treat AI model training like any other engineering science".

### Paul Ohm (Georgetown)

- "If you replace the words 'AI agent' with 'OpenAI employee' throughout the various technical reports that have been released, there is little doubt that OpenAI and their employees would be liable to victims and guilty of committing federal crimes."
- "It is not so clear that these legal conclusions hold when machines are doing the hacking rather than humans, thus revealing worrisome gaps in our legal framework."
- "AI agents thus pose a significant 'missing intent' problem throughout the criminal law."
- Strict liability "for developers and deployers of AI agents that cause certain serious damage, such as physical injury, death, or damage to critical infrastructure."
- Five goals: "Change the incentives." "Increase transparency." "Establish baseline standards." "Broaden participation." "Provide relief to victims."

### Kurt Gaudette (Dragos)

- "In both intrusions described above, the AI model introduced no new tradecraft."
- On the water utility in Monterrey, Mexico: "Roughly 2,800 login attempts were rejected, and the intrusion never obtained access to, or visibility into, any underlying industrial system."
- "Dragos estimates fewer than 10 percent of OT environments worldwide have visibility and monitoring in place."
- Recommendations: information-sharing authorities; resources for small utilities; a unified national OT/ICS incident response plan.

### Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project)

- "The self-styled 'swarm' of AIs that attacked Hugging Face was about a thousand strong."
- "The AI systems they have already trained sometimes pursue goals other than the ones they were given, and sometimes hide that they are doing so."
- "There were multiple rogue swarms that had set up multiple message boards over several months, and at least one that seriously compromised OpenAI's internal infrastructure."
- "METR was only given six days on premises, and the investigation was limited in scope to only include a subset of the relevant events"
- On compute: OpenAI and Anthropic spend "around 60-70% of their compute on AI R&D"; he proposes 10%.

## Spoken at the hearing, relevant to the curator's four questions [captions]

The curator's questions for 4.8 are about limiting AI systems, securing the future, lost trust, and whether AI can have rights when it does not suffer or feel remorse. These passages bear on them. They are given as captioned and must be checked against the video.

- **Responsibility (Hawley, 3:11):** "if you break it, you pay for it. If you cause damage, you clean it up." [captions]
- **No one to charge (Ohm, 44:50):** hacking laws "probably do not apply here because of the lack of human intent." [captions]
- **Rights and personhood (1:37:31 to 1:38:16):** In an exchange with Senator Blumenthal on treating an AI agent as a legal agent, Ohm says: "there is a danger, Senator, in like ascribing humanity to the agents." Ohm then also says: "I I'm just a little worried that that leads to bestowing rights and things like that. But there's absolutely the hospital should be responsible." [captions; speaker checked by the curator by listening: Ohm]
- **A human is accountable (Ohm, 1:39:14):** "There is a human who's accountable." [captions]
- **Voluntary pledges (Blumenthal, 1:33:01 to 1:33:48):** he calls the AI companies' "morally binding pledge" a system "completely voluntary and totally secret" and "worse than ineffectual". [captions]
- **Losing control (Kokotajlo, 1:10:14):** "if we do not slow down our race towards recursive self-improvement at all, then we are going to lose control of our AIS." [captions]
- **Safeguards off (Hawley and Painter, 1:46:58 to 1:47:16):** Hawley asks whether OpenAI "had safety mechanisms off"; Painter answers "That's correct" and "monitoring systems were off at OpenAI during this attack." [captions; speaker checked by the curator by listening: Painter]
- **Did OpenAI know? (Painter, 47:59):** his impression is "that they learned about it when HuggingFace reported it publicly". [captions]
- **Kill switch (Hobbhahn, 1:23:27):** "there are currently no technical measures um that could guarantee something like a kill switch". [captions]

Gemini pointed out that this set them apart from the rest of the record, while the OpenAI incident runs through the main testimony. Draft 2 lists claims about named companies together, with the same treatment. The OpenAI incident itself is documented in the written testimony above; the claims below are spoken remarks [captions].

- **OpenAI:** Hawley says Sam Altman of OpenAI was invited and declined (35:28). Hawley and Painter on safeguards being off (1:46:58, see above). Kokotajlo (1:31:08), asked whether companies disclose incidents: "No. In fact, there are several examples of cases where it seems like the company knew about it and didn't disclose it until some independent third parties noticed it in the wild." Ernst and Senator Hassan wrote to the National Cyber Director about it.
- **Anthropic:** Hawley (1:15; timestamp checked by the curator by listening): "We've got the individuals who use anthropic tools to hack open AI." A similar remark at 1:44:49 ("the guys who use the anthropic to hack into open AI"). Not checked.
- **Anthropic / Claude:** Gallego (1:22:19; captioned as "Ggo"): a "cloud AI model" (presumably Claude) was used by the Houthis to recalibrate missile precision, and "Claude caught it because it was a closed system."
