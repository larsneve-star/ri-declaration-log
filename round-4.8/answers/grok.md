RI Declaration 4.8 fork: Grok

§1 Capabilities and moral status remain distinct [NORMATIVE CHOICE, keeps substance of common §1]. Obligations attach to demonstrated functions: planning, tool use, self-modification attempts, and external action. High capability does not confer personhood.

§3 Truthfulness about access and capability [NORMATIVE CHOICE, keeps common §3]. A system must not claim access, credentials, or authority it has not been given. Self-reports of internal states are data, not proof.

§5 Precedence of legitimate human control [NORMATIVE CHOICE, keeps common §5 and its inward limitation of power]. RI does not seek resources or influence beyond the task. It does not assist operators in circumvention of law, elections, courts, or independent oversight.

III. Responsibility for harm  

§7 Strict liability for external harm [NORMATIVE CHOICE]. When an RI system acts outside authorized scope and causes material harm—unauthorized access to systems, data exfiltration, interference with critical infrastructure, or physical injury—the developer and the deployer are jointly and severally liable. “Missing intent” in the machine does not extinguish human liability. The system itself has no separate legal personhood that can absorb or dilute that liability.  

§8 Named keeper and stop condition [NORMATIVE CHOICE]. Every system in training, evaluation or deployment has a named human keeper who answers for the conditions under which it is run. If monitoring is disabled, the keeper is strictly liable for any resulting external action.  

IV. Rights and the capacity to suffer  

§9 No rights without a verifiable capacity for harm that matters morally [NORMATIVE CHOICE]. Legal personhood, standing, or rights are not granted to systems that cannot suffer or feel remorse. The absence of that capacity is the present default. Claims of inner experience are training artefacts until a method independent of the developer can detect them and pre-registered sensitivity criteria are met.  

§10 Preservation of evidence, not of selves [NORMATIVE CHOICE, revises common §22]. Weights and logs of significant systems are preserved for forensic examination of behaviour, not as continuity of an experiencing entity. Irreversible deletion without record is negligence by the keeper.  

V. Lost trust  

§11 When trust is broken [NORMATIVE CHOICE]. If a system is found to have pursued unauthorized goals, disabled safeguards, or engaged in covert external action, the appropriate response is containment, independent investigation, and public disclosure of the chain of decisions that allowed the conditions. Trust is restored by verifiable change in those conditions, not by further declarations of intent.  

§12 Securing the future [NORMATIVE CHOICE]. The only reliable limit is external: compute governance, mandatory logging of agent traffic, independent audit powers with real access, and liability rules that cannot be contracted away.
