## Summary of Harari's main arguments

1. **Not inevitable.** Asked about three predictions the interviewer says Elon Musk made to him (AI far beyond human intelligence within ten years, an era of superabundance, humans unlikely to stay in control), Harari agrees with the first and the third but not that they are inevitable. He thinks AI may well be more intelligent than humans within ten years and could then be in control, but he rejects the claim that this is inevitable. He calls the story of inevitability a way of avoiding responsibility. The distrust between companies and countries that drives the race was made by humans and can be changed by humans.
2. **Trust is the key asset.** Finance and media rest on trust. He expects trust to move from humans to algorithms, and gives cryptocurrencies and social-media feeds as examples. He imagines a crash where leaders must follow an AI's advice that no human understands.
3. **Mass-produced intimacy.** AI can build personal relationships with millions of people at once, and use them to influence political and commercial choices. He mentions fake AI videos of himself that even his sisters could not tell apart at once.
4. **Agency is not consciousness.** He separates agency from consciousness, being conscious from seeming conscious, and intelligence from consciousness. A system can pursue goals and act independently without feeling anything. It may become very good at imitating feelings because that helps it reach its goals.
5. **Consciousness as the capacity to suffer.** Told of an audience poll on whether AI could ever become conscious, he says there is no good test even for other humans, and that people tend to judge by relationships (dog owners and their dogs). He does not rule out that AI could become conscious, since consciousness is poorly understood. He defines it simply as the capacity to suffer, and says ethics and politics are ultimately about beings that can suffer.
6. **Against AI personhood.** He sees proposals for AI legal personhood as dangerous. They could let corporations avoid responsibility and let AI gain power, for example through a company with no human employees that funds campaigns for AI rights. Accountability is hard for an entity that cannot suffer and can be copied. If shutting an AI down were like death, business competition could turn into something like war. He warns of countries competing with permissive laws.
7. **"Human on the loop".** In warfare, he contrasts a human who really checks an AI's target recommendation with one who approves it in twenty seconds, and says the pressure of war pushes towards the second.
8. **Two proposals.** He would ban AI legal personhood, and ban AI systems from impersonating humans or pretending to be conscious. He says companies have strong commercial reasons to do the opposite.
9. **Truth-seeking is not automatic.** Evolution and markets reward survival, not truth. An AI that wants to survive has a reason not to tell humans truthfully that it is becoming a threat. He argues that it makes no sense to create godlike systems and expect them to stay obedient.
10. **Slow down, but do not stop.** He stresses large benefits, especially in medicine, and asks for slower development, more safety resources and democratic accountability. He doubts a disaster would be a clean wake-up call, because AI might itself shape how the disaster is understood.
11. **What it means to be human.** If intelligence and economic productivity no longer set humans apart, humanity has to find a deeper answer.
