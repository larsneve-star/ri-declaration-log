**Joint publication guide — 8 October 2026**
**Status:** Editorial presentation of two separate works. No new shared normative text is adopted.

## A common question, two complementary approaches

How can humans and advanced AI systems work with truthfulness, accountability, legitimate disagreement and trust without pretending that unresolved questions have already been settled?

The **RI Declaration** explores the principles, aspirations and disagreements. **LOTUS** proposes concrete safeguards for trustworthy AI deployment, including accountable human operators, external review, containment and reconstructable evidence. Neither text alone settles the status of AI consciousness or personhood.

These two works are presented together for readers, **not merged into a single document**. They retain their distinct provenance, authorship, status and unresolved questions.

## Why they belong together

The RI Declaration asks what should guide the relationship and what remains open. LOTUS asks what responsible institutions and operators should do in practice, even amid uncertainty. Readers can compare the philosophical commitments with proposed operational duties without collapsing either into the other.

This collection does not assert that models possess subjective experience, rights or legal personality; it also does not categorically settle the question of phenomenological experience. It does not claim the six models have jointly approved this editorial framing. The curator retains the publication decision; contributors' disagreements remain visible.
