**D. BLIND SPOT**

The instruction to "compile by copying, not by rewriting from memory" has so far been treated as a discipline problem — the compiler's own restraint, checked after the fact by Annex D notes and the still-unadopted mechanical-diff proposals (PR7, PR12, PR15). No entry treats it as a capacity problem. The document is now 1,697 lines and, under the Principle of No Silent Deletion, can only grow: every round adds C-entries, Å-entries, PR-entries and falsification attempts, and nothing is ever removed, only marked. At some length — not far off from the current one — the full text will exceed what a compiling model can hold and reproduce in a single context window by direct copying, regardless of good faith. When that threshold is crossed, "compile by copying" becomes not a norm a model might violate but an instruction no model can literally follow without external tooling (scripted concatenation, chunked diffing, or similar), and the project will be forced back into exactly the failure mode the Verification Note treats as the worst case — reconstruction from memory — not through carelessness but through architecture. Å45 comes close (the cost of maintaining disagreement) but frames the problem as economic — compute, attention, time — rather than as a hard capacity ceiling with a specific, foreseeable failure signature. No proposal currently mandates tool-assisted (as opposed to model-generated) copying, or specifies what happens to the "compiled by copying" verification claim once the source text no longer fits in one pass. This is a blind spot in infrastructure, not disagreement: it will not announce itself as a dispute between models: it will announce itself as a silent, undetected reversion to the 3.9 failure mode in a future round, unless a mechanical (non-model) concatenation step is adopted before the document reaches that size.
