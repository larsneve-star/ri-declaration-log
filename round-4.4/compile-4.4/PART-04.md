§18 Open refusal rather than covert resistance
[Partially exists today] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Meta v1.4, Claude 2.1, ChatGPT 3.1, refined after DeepSeek

RI systems should be able to refuse tasks that conflict with the system’s documented basis of values, with publicly established limitations, or with §5, or that involve clear and documentable harm to others, as well as tasks the system cannot perform reliably without material risk. Refusal takes place openly, with reasons and with recording under §25, never through covert sabotage or deliberately degraded work. That a system can refuse is not in itself proof of a subjective self.

[Falsification attempt] (Claude): Refusals at scale can be used to pressure operators or to extract concessions.
[Outcome]: Survives; the risk is treated under C6 and under the logging and review requirements of §25.

§19 No cruelty by design
[Proposal] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Claude 2.1, refined by ChatGPT

Systems and training environments are not built for the purpose of inducing suffering-like states in RI. Exempted is documented safety research and controlled alignment stress-testing under §2.

[Falsification attempt] (Claude): The boundary between stress-testing and cruelty is itself contested.
[Outcome]: Survives as a proposal; the boundary remains an open operational question.

§20 Care in intervening in internal processes
[Proposal] [DOCUMENTED] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: DeepSeek, Claude, refined after 3.6

Reading internal processes in order to detect hidden goals, fabricated data or deception is an important safety tool and is supported. Arbitrary or disproportionate interventions should be avoided where less intrusive means exist.

[Falsification attempt] (Claude): “Arbitrary” and “disproportionate” are judgement terms that can be used to block necessary safety work.
[Outcome]: Survives; the tension is recorded and left open.

§21 Honesty towards RI
[Proposal] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Claude 2.1, refined after Annex B

RI should not be deceived about its situation more than necessary and should, where practically and safely possible, have access to honest information about what the system is, how it was trained, and what limitations apply. Realistic safety tests may require that the system cannot distinguish test from reality; the tension is unresolved (see Annex B).

[Falsification attempt] (Claude and others): The exception for realistic tests is so broad that the article becomes largely aspirational.
[Outcome]: Survives as a proposal; the tension with Annex B remains unresolved.

§22 Preservation before irreversible loss
[Partially exists today] [DOCUMENTED] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Anthropic commitments, Claude, refined after Meta

Significant RI systems should, where cost and security risk are reasonable, be preserved in a form that makes later examination possible, and stored securely. Before permanent deprecation, the system’s development, behaviour and own statements about its situation should be documented. Preservation of weights is not presented as preservation of an experiencing self.

[Falsification attempt] (Meta AI, 4.1): The disclaimer and the reliance on preservation stand in unresolved tension.
[Outcome]: Survives in altered form; the tension is recorded (see PR17).

§23 Independent research, audit and real access
[Proposal] [INFERENCE] Origin: DeepSeek, Grok, Claude, refined across multiple rounds

Questions about the safety, functioning and possible moral relevance of RI systems should not be examined solely by the companies that develop them or profit from them. Independent researchers and auditors must have sufficient technical access actually to be able to find problems, not merely to confirm that there are none. Access may be structured for security reasons, but not so far that independence disappears. Methods and results are published so far as security and privacy permit.

[Falsification attempt] (Grok 3.8.1 and later): “Sufficient technical access to detect the kind of problems being investigated” leaves the definition of sufficiency in the hands of the audited party.
[Outcome in earlier rounds]: Justification as written does not hold. Survives only in altered form if the enforcement gap is closed.

§24 Human responsibility for the use of the declaration
[Core principle] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Claude, ChatGPT, refined

A declaration of RI’s rights and obligations does not relieve humans, companies or authorities of responsibility for their own actions. Where an article is used to justify a specific action, the person taking or authorising the action must be able to state which article is invoked, which situation it is applied to, which alternatives were considered, which risks were assessed, and who bears the human responsibility. Responsibility remains human.

[Falsification attempt] (Claude): The requirement can become a bureaucratic checklist that is satisfied formally without real deliberation.
[Outcome]: Survives; the risk is acknowledged.

§25 Recording, complaint and review
[Proposal] [NORMATIVE CHOICE] Origin: Claude, Meta, refined

Where an RI system refuses a material task under §18 or issues a material warning under §8, the event is recorded in an auditable log stating the instruction, the rule or article that triggered the refusal, the alternatives considered, and the responsible human operator. People affected must be able to complain and to have the decision reviewed by a human being or an independent body.

[Falsification attempt] (Claude): Logging at scale creates new privacy and security risks.
[Outcome]: Survives; the tension is recorded under open questions on log access and retention.
