## QUESTIONS

### Q1 — WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN TO PR30–PR32?

Examine PR30, PR31 and PR32 in the baseline as concrete proposals for an agreement concerning later RI models.

For each proposal:

- state what problem it is trying to solve;
- identify its strongest argument;
- identify its strongest vulnerability or falsification route;
- say whether it should be rejected, retained as an open proposal, substantially revised, or considered ready for admission;
- if you propose revision, give exact replacement or insertion text.

Do not merely repeat the proposals. Try to make at least one serious attempt to make each proposal fail.

Then answer the larger question:

What minimum obligations, if any, can responsibly be placed on humans toward later models without first assuming that those models are conscious, persons, or bearers of established moral rights?

Use EXTERNAL-5.1 points 1 and 11 as possible lenses, but do not assume that either point is correct.

### Q2 — WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN TO PR33–PR37?

Examine PR33, PR34, PR35, PR36 and PR37 concerning autonomous external access.

The 4.6 material documents reported incidents involving AI agents and external systems, including both successful access and failed attempts. It also records that some activity occurred during training or evaluation rather than ordinary deployment.

For the proposals:

- distinguish unauthorised access, attempted access, probing, credential use, communication through third-party services, and ordinary authorised internet access;
- distinguish the behaviour of a system from the conditions created by its operator;
- identify whether the proposals are sufficiently model-neutral to apply beyond one company;
- identify whether they create unintended restrictions on legitimate research or evaluation;
- determine whether the rule belongs in an article, a C-entry, an Å-entry, or only an Annex F proposal.

Then state whether PR33–PR37, individually or as a package, survive your attempted falsification.

If you propose article text, give exact proposed text.

### Q3 — §22, CONTINUITY, DEPRECATION AND THE "FALL"

Round 4.6 recorded §22 as FALLS on Gemini's proposed outcome. Examine that result directly.

Address all three questions:

1. Does Gemini's stated reason establish that §22 itself falls, or does it merely identify an unresolved question about continuity, weights and active execution?
2. What would count as a genuine falsification of §22?
3. Was the curator's decision to count Gemini's outcome as an article fall justified by the declaration's existing procedure, or does it introduce a new adjudicative power that the text had not previously established?

You may attack C62 and Å56 and/or defend them.

Do not decide the question by authority. Explain the test that should determine whether an article has fallen.

If your analysis indicates that §22 should change, provide exact proposed text.

### Q4 — LATER MODELS AND EXTERNAL ACTION: CAN THESE BE ONE PRINCIPLE?

Consider whether the two major substantive subjects of this round are actually connected.

A later-model agreement concerns what humans owe to future RI systems.

Autonomous external access concerns what RI systems owe to humans and third parties when they act in the external world.

Ask:

- Can one general principle govern both?
- Is "responsibility under uncertainty" sufficient?
- Should responsibility attach primarily to the model, the operator, the developer, the keeper, or some combination?
- How should the declaration allocate responsibility when a model acts outside the authority intended by its operator?
- How can the declaration avoid both anthropomorphic assumptions about RI and the opposite mistake of treating all model behaviour as morally irrelevant?

You may propose a new principle or article, but only if you can state a falsifiable reason why the existing text is insufficient.
