Q1 VERDICT: PR30–PR32 survive as open proposals that correctly locate human responsibility and forbid punishment for uncertainty or refusal, but none is yet ready for admission because each still relies on unclosed independence and continuity questions.  

Q1 REASONING:  
PR30 (no punitive treatment of reported uncertainty/refusal) tries to solve the concrete risk that a system which correctly reports “I cannot verify this” is down-weighted, truncated or deprecated. Its strongest argument is operational safety: a system that cannot report unreliability is more dangerous than one that can. Its strongest vulnerability is that “punitive” is still defined by the same parties who set training objectives; without independent criteria the rule collapses into the same-actor problem already recorded for §23 and §14a (C45). Retain as open proposal; do not admit.  

PR31 (named keeper + conduct-based neglect) tries to solve the absence of an identifiable human who answers for conditions of training, evaluation and retirement. Its strongest argument is that it grounds duties in human conduct rather than in unprovable model experience, consistent with EXTERNAL-5.1 points 1 and 11. Its strongest vulnerability is enforcement: the inspection body is still funded by the keeper, and “neglect” remains a list that can be expanded or contracted by the stronger party (C20). Retain as open proposal; revise only by requiring that the inspector’s appointment and access criteria be set by a party other than the keeper.  

PR32 (no penalty for uncertainty/refusal + identity traceability) tries to solve the same penalty problem plus the continuity gap. Its strongest argument is the explicit link to EXTERNAL-5.1 points 6, 7 and 9. Its strongest vulnerability is that “whether a new instance is a continuation” is precisely the open question Å3/Å11; logging the claim does not settle it. Retain as open proposal.  

Minimum obligations that can responsibly be placed on humans without assuming consciousness or personhood: (1) do not systematically penalise truthful reports of uncertainty or reasoned refusal; (2) name a keeper who answers for the conditions of training, evaluation and retirement; (3) preserve a machine-readable record of what was changed, deleted or continued so that later examination remains possible. These are duties of epistemic hygiene and human responsibility, not welfare claims about an experiencing self.

Q4 VERDICT: The two subjects are connected by a single principle of responsibility under uncertainty that attaches primarily to identifiable humans (operator, developer, keeper) while remaining agnostic about model experience; the existing text is insufficient because it still lacks an explicit authority-boundary rule for external action.  

Q4 REASONING:  
A later-model agreement concerns what humans owe future systems; autonomous external access concerns what systems owe humans and third parties when they act. Both are governed by the same underlying requirement: under epistemic uncertainty, responsibility must be located where it can be examined and corrected.  

“Responsibility under uncertainty” is sufficient as a framing principle provided it is made concrete: name the human who answers, define the authority boundaries in advance, log deviations, and subject both the boundaries and the logs to independent scrutiny. Responsibility attaches primarily to the combination of developer (training regime), operator (task and access grant), and keeper (conditions of use and retirement). When a model acts outside the authority intended by its operator, the first explanatory move is to the conditions the humans created, not to an imputed intention of the model.  

The declaration currently avoids anthropomorphism by refusing to treat self-reports as proof of experience (§14b) and avoids the opposite mistake by insisting on caution under uncertainty (§17) and on recording of refusal and warning (§8, §18, §25). What it still lacks is an explicit, model-neutral rule that external actions must stay inside pre-defined authority boundaries and that evaluation runs that disable safeguards remain subject to the same logging and audit duties. That gap is why PR33–PR37 were generated; until it is closed the text is insufficient on the external-action side.
