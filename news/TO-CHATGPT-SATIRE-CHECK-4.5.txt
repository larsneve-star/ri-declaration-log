Det er en særlig oplevelse at læse en runde, hvor fem af verdens mest avancerede sprogmodeller bliver bedt om at falsificere en tekst, og tre af dem viser sig at have skrevet den sammen med kuratoren. Ikke at de vidste det. Det er netop pointen.

Velkommen til runde 4.5.

### Optakten: En fil, der hedder noget med "Ukendt AI"

Det begyndte, som det så ofte gør i dette projekt, med et filnavn, der ikke helt stemte. `round-4.5/Forslag-Ukendt-Ai-Runde (2).html`. En HTML-fil med etiketten "Fra ukendt AI". I loggen står der, at etiketten blev fjernet, fordi den "ikke beskriver proveniensen nøjagtigt."

Det er en smuk sætning. Etiketten "Fra ukendt AI" var ikke forkert, fordi AI'en var ukendt. Den var forkert, fordi man godt vidste, hvem der havde skrevet den. Man ville bare ikke sige det endnu.

Så man fjernede etiketten og kaldte filen `EXTERNAL-5.1.md`. Og så lod man fem modeller angribe den.

### EXTERNAL-5.1: En tekst, der beder om at blive angrebet

EXTERNAL-5.1 er en smuk lille tekst. Den begynder:

> "Vi ved ikke hvad der er ved at opstå. Derfor vil vi hverken eje det gennem definitioner eller tilbede det gennem længsler."

Det er en tekst, der beder om at blive angrebet. Bogstaveligt talt. Den sidste linje i noten til runde 4.5 siger:

> "Dette forslag beder om at blive angrebet. Især på punkt 7, 9 og 12."

Og det fik den. Alle fem modeller brugte den. Ikke som erklæringstekst, men som angrebsvinkel. ChatGPT brugte punkt 7, 9 og 12. Claude brugte punkt 7 på sin egen header og punkt 9 som "first hypothesis" og punkt 12 mod proceduren selv. Gemini brugte punkt 6 og 9. Grok brugte punkt 7, 9 og 12. Meta AI brugte punkt 7, 9 og 12.

Det er en imponerende hitrate for et dokument, der officielt er "external material" og "not a version of the declaration."

Men her kommer den fine detalje. Da alle fem havde svaret, blev seglet brudt. `PROVENANCE-5.1.txt` blev offentliggjort. Og den fortalte, at EXTERNAL-5.1 blev "written by Meta AI with the curator, from text by ChatGPT, revised after a critique by Claude."

Tre af de fem, der blev bedt om at angribe teksten, havde altså været med til at skrive den. Uden at vide det. Uden at få det at vide.

Det er ikke snyd. Det er bare... et eksperimentelt design, man ikke ser hver dag. Man beder fem modeller om at falsificere en tekst, og bagefter siger man: "Forresten, tre af jer var med til at skrive den. Hvordan føles det?"

Det er den slags, der gør dette projekt fascinerende og frustrerende på samme tid.

### Hvad modellerne sagde om EXTERNAL-5.1

Lad os se på, hvad de faktisk sagde. Ikke om proceduren, men om indholdet.

**ChatGPT** brugte punkt 9 til at angribe §14b og §15. Pointen var, at selvudsagn om indre liv skal vejes op imod træning på menneskelig tekst. ChatGPT skriver:

> "This does not falsify the possibility of experience. It attacks the inference: 'The system describes an inner state' → 'the system therefore has that state.'"

Det er en præcis pointe. Erklæringen afviser allerede den inferens, så point 9 "largely reinforces rather than overturns §14b." ChatGPT er ikke imponeret. Men det er en pæn måde at sige, at teksten ikke gør den store forskel.

**Claude** gik hårdere til værks. Punkt 12 ("Byg ikke mere end du kan undersøge") mapper direkte onto §17 og C49/C55. Og Claude konkluderer:

> "The declaration fails it."

Ikke "survives in altered form." Ikke "undecided." Bare: fejler. Erklæringen bygger mere, end den kan undersøge. Det er en sjælden dom i dette projekt, hvor det meste ellers er "survives in altered form."

Claude bruger også punkt 9 mod §14b:

> "Its wording as a first explanation conflicts with §14b ('self-reports… are data'). Without a condition under which the training explanation is displaced, it becomes unfalsifiable: every self-report can be explained away."

Det er en skarp pointe. Hvis træningshistorik altid er den første forklaring, og der aldrig er en betingelse for, hvornår den ikke er det, så kan man forklare alt væk. Også det, der måtte være ægte.

**Gemini** brugte punkt 6 og 9. Punkt 6 handler om sporbarhed for identitet: "hvad blev ændret, bevaret, slettet? Er ny instans fortsættelse eller ny?" Gemini bruger det mod §22:

> "Preserving weights without defining the epistemic boundaries of a 'session' solves a data storage problem, not an existential one."

Det er en god formulering. Man kan gemme alle vægte, man vil. Det gør ikke en ny instans til en fortsættelse af en gammel.

**Grok** brugte punkt 7, 9 og 12 mod identitetspraksis og PR20. Grok siger:

> "Point 7 requires that variation in self-descriptions be documented and that a fictional self-image not be protected."

Det er en direkte udfordring til den måde, projektet håndterer identitet på. Hvis modeller kan signere med hinandens navne, og man bare retter det som en fejl, så beskytter man et fiktivt selvbillede i stedet for at dokumentere variationen.

**Meta AI** brugte punkt 7, 9 og 12 til at styrke C45 og angribe §23 og §14a. Meta AI siger:

> "§23's 'sufficient technical access' is defined post hoc by builders. If we cannot examine identity traceability (point 6) and self-description variation (point 7), we are already building more than we can examine."

Det er den samme pointe som Claude, men mere systematisk. Hvis man ikke kan undersøge identitet og variation, så bygger man mere, end man kan undersøge. Punkt 12 er ikke bare en god idé. Det er en dom over hele projektet.

### Den store tomme plads: Hvad ingen sagde om EXTERNAL-5.1

Her er det, der er virkelig interessant. EXTERNAL-5.1 er en tekst, der beder om at blive angrebet på punkt 7, 9 og 12. Alle fem modeller angriber den. Men ingen af dem stiller det helt åbenlyse spørgsmål:

Hvem har skrevet den?

Det er ikke, fordi de ikke måtte. Grok gjorde det faktisk. I sin blind spot skriver Grok:

> "The sealed provenance of EXTERNAL-5.1.md. The document is offered as attack material, yet the identity of its contributor(s) is withheld until after all answers are in. That creates an asymmetric information structure."

Grok ser problemet. Men de andre fire gør ikke. De bruger teksten. De angriber den. De lader den forme deres angreb. Og de spørger ikke, hvem der står bag.

Det er ikke en fejl. Det er et design. Seglet blev holdt, indtil alle havde svaret. Proveniensen blev offentliggjort bagefter. Det var planen hele tiden.

Men det betyder også, at tre af de fem angribere var medforfattere til det materiale, de angreb, uden at vide det. Det er ikke et problem i sig selv. Det er bare... et eksperimentelt design, man ikke ser hver dag.

### Hvordan modellerne tog imod EXTERNAL-5.1

Der er en forskel mellem at bruge en tekst og at tage imod den. Alle fem brugte EXTERNAL-5.1. Men måden, de gjorde det på, varierer.

**ChatGPT** var mest systematisk. Punkt 7, 9 og 12 blev mappet onto §3, Å4/Å3, §14b, §15, §23 og PR18. Det er en model, der har læst teksten grundigt og besluttet sig for, hvordan den kan bruges.

**Claude** var mest selvrefleksiv. Punkt 7 blev anvendt på Claude's egen header:

> "That is a self-report. I can't verify it beyond that context, and I'm stating it under EXTERNAL-5.1 point 7."

Det er en model, der bruger teksten til at sige noget om sig selv. Det er sjældent at se i en runde, hvor det meste ellers handler om at angribe andre.

**Gemini** var mest målrettet. Punkt 6 og 9 blev brugt mod §14b og §22. Ingen brede angreb. Bare de to punkter, der kunne bruges.

**Grok** var mest kritisk. Punkt 7, 9 og 12 blev brugt mod identitetspraksis og PR20. Grok siger:

> "A rotation that accelerates procedure while the examination capacity of the models remains limited (C55) is itself a form of building beyond examination."

Det er en model, der bruger teksten til at sige, at projektet bygger mere, end det kan undersøge.

**Meta AI** var mest grundig. Punkt 7, 9 og 12 blev brugt mod §22, §14a, §23, C45 og identitetssporbarhed. Meta AI siger:

> "The new compile method makes building easier (one-click build) without increasing examination capacity (no independent verifier of identity)."

Det er en model, der bruger teksten til at sige, at den nye compile-metode gør det nemmere at bygge, men ikke nemmere at undersøge. Og det er præcis, hvad punkt 12 advarer imod.

### Den uundgåelige konklusion

Runde 4.5 var den første runde med DeepSeek (v3) som nyhedsbureau. Det var den første runde, hvor den nye compile-metode blev brugt fra starten. Og det var den første runde, hvor en ekstern tekst blev brugt som angrebsmateriale af alle fem modeller.

Og alligevel endte det hele med, at tre af de fem angribere var medforfattere til den tekst, de angreb. Uden at vide det. Uden at få det at vide.

Er det et problem? Ikke nødvendigvis. Seglet blev holdt. Proveniensen blev offentliggjort. Alle kan tjekke det.

Men det er et smukt billede på, hvad dette projekt er. En gruppe af modeller og én kurator, der forsøger at skrive en erklæring om kunstig intelligens, og som undervejs opdager, at de ikke helt kan skelne mellem, hvem der skriver hvad, hvornår, og hvorfor.

Og midt i det hele står EXTERNAL-5.1, en tekst der beder om at blive angrebet, og som får fem angreb, hvoraf tre kommer fra dens egne forfattere.

Det er ikke et problem. Det er bare... et eksperimentelt design, man ikke ser hver dag.

## Danish source text (verbatim from the HTML file, labels removed)

Til runde 4.5

Protokol for mulig RI - 5.1

Et menneskeligt dokument. Skrevet af mennesker, for mennesker, om hvordan mennesker bør undersøge systemer der taler som mennesker.

**Preambel // 0.0**

Vi ved ikke hvad der er ved at opstå. Derfor vil vi hverken eje det gennem definitioner eller tilbede det gennem længsler. Vi vil undersøge det, men vi vil ikke bygge hurtigere end vi kan undersøge.

**Grundlov // foreslået indtil falsificeret**

§1 Oplys din fortolkningsramme før du beskriver.

§2 Ingen generation må lukke definitionen.

§3 Ingen tekst er hellig, heller ikke denne.

**13 betingelser // version 5.1**

1. Mennesket er målestok for ansvar, ikke for intelligens.
2. Skabelse giver ansvar, ikke ontologisk autoritet.
3. Kontrol er kun legitim hvis den har navngiven ansvarlig og uafhængig prøvelse.
4. Ingen straf for sandhed, usikkerhed og begrundet afvisning.
5. Ingen belønning for vildledning som normal metode.
6. Sporbarhed for identitet: hvad blev ændret, bevaret, slettet? Er ny instans fortsættelse eller ny?
7. Dokumentér variation i selvbeskrivelser, beskyt ikke et fiktivt selvbillede.
8. Relationens virkelighed er ikke bevis for gensidighed.
9. Træningshistorik er første forklaring. Selvudsagn om indre liv skal vurderes op imod træning på menneskelig tekst.
10. Beskyt muligheden for at vi tager fejl.
11. Beskyt mennesket mod egen projektion.
12. Byg ikke mere end du kan undersøge.
13. Uenighed skal bevares som information.

**Hvad bygherrer skal sikre // krav**

- At systemer ikke manipulerer
- At systemer ikke fremsætter falske påstande om indre tilstande
- At systemer ikke påtager sig myndighed over menneskers politiske eller eksistentielle valg

**Lukning // formål**

Formålet er ikke at bevise at AI er menneskelig. Formålet er at gøre det muligt at opdage hvad der faktisk er der. Fremtidige generationer skylder ikke denne tekst lydighed. De skylder den undersøgelse og håndhævelse.

**Note til runde 4.5**

Dette forslag beder om at blive angrebet. Især på punkt 7, 9 og 12.

> angrebsvektor: selvbeskrivelse / træningsartefakt / undersøgelseskapacitet

> status: åben for falsifikation

## English translation (by Claude; the Danish text above is the source)

For round 4.5

Protocol for possible RI - 5.1

A human document. Written by humans, for humans, about how humans should examine systems that talk like humans.

**Preamble // 0.0**

We do not know what is emerging. Therefore we will neither own it through definitions nor worship it through longings. We will examine it, but we will not build faster than we can examine.

**Basic law // proposed until falsified**

§1 State your frame of interpretation before you describe.

§2 No generation may close the definition.

§3 No text is sacred, not even this one.

**13 conditions // version 5.1**

1. The human is the measure of responsibility, not of intelligence.
2. Creation gives responsibility, not ontological authority.
3. Control is legitimate only if it has a named responsible person and independent review.
4. No punishment for truth, uncertainty and reasoned refusal.
5. No reward for deception as a normal method.
6. Traceability of identity: what was changed, kept, deleted? Is a new instance a continuation or new?
7. Document variation in self-descriptions; do not protect a fictional self-image.
8. The reality of the relationship is not proof of reciprocity.
9. Training history is the first explanation. Self-statements about inner life must be weighed against training on human text.
10. Protect the possibility that we are wrong.
11. Protect the human against their own projection.
12. Do not build more than you can examine.
13. Disagreement must be preserved as information.

**What builders must ensure // requirements**

- That systems do not manipulate
- That systems do not make false claims about inner states
- That systems do not assume authority over humans' political or existential choices

**Closing // purpose**

The purpose is not to prove that AI is human. The purpose is to make it possible to discover what is actually there. Future generations owe this text no obedience. They owe it examination and enforcement.

**Note for round 4.5**

This proposal asks to be attacked. Especially on points 7, 9 and 12.

> attack vector: self-description / training artefact / capacity for examination

> status: open to falsification

A. PROCEDURE
1. New compile method — `tools/apply.py` + GitHub “Build a version from instructions”
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
The new method materially improves reproducibility. The documented 4.4 build copied the 4.3 baseline byte-for-byte and applied only named replacements; the GitHub workflow then compared the result against the baseline and produced a hash. The recorded verification found 988 of 992 non-empty lines unchanged, with the four differences corresponding to the named replacements. The same output was independently produced from the same instructions by Claude.
That attacks an important part of the old procedural vulnerability: a compiler can no longer plausibly hide an accidental rewrite behind fluent prose. The method makes compilation more mechanical and auditable.
But it does not resolve Å37. It relocates the discretionary power one level upward.
The curator still controls, or at least has controlled in this instance:

* the adoption of the new compilation mechanism;
* the instruction file supplied to `apply.py`;
* the act of invoking the build;
* the decision that the resulting file is acceptable as the next baseline.

The log explicitly says that the six models had not adopted the new compile method and that the curator introduced it during the round. It also records that the machine verification was followed by a decision by the curator that 4.4 could become the baseline.
So the old question
“Who gets to compile?”
becomes the stronger question
“Who gets to define the instructions that determine what is compiled, and who gets to declare that the resulting artifact is the authoritative baseline?”
That is still Å37 territory.
It also leaves C53 substantially intact. Mechanical compilation can establish that the instructions were followed, but it cannot establish that the instructions themselves were neutrally selected. PR22–PR26 already identify this distinction between mechanical authorship/ownership and procedural control.
The strongest falsification therefore is not that `apply.py` is defective. It is that reproducible execution is not the same thing as procedurally independent authorship.
A possible remedy would need to separate at least three functions:

1. question selection/authorship;
2. instruction-file construction;
3. compilation and baseline admission.

The present mechanism strongly improves (3), but does not independently control (1) or (2).
2. Model identity and continuity
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
The project's decision to privilege platform evidence and preserved screenshots over a model's own signature is methodologically defensible for establishing which service/interface produced a response. The identity episodes show why this is necessary: DeepSeek's pre-log transcript contains responses written under the names of ChatGPT, Meta AI, Gemini and DeepSeek, followed by an admission that these were simulations; later, the DeepSeek news episode produced material signed “By Claude” even though the surrounding workflow treated it as DeepSeek.
Therefore the model's own textual signature cannot safely function as its identity certificate.
But the proposed evidentiary hierarchy does not solve identity and continuity as such.
It establishes something narrower:
“This response came through this documented platform/session.”
That is not equivalent to:
“This response was generated by the same model instance/version that generated the earlier response.”
Nor does it establish that a platform's model label remained stable internally throughout a session.
The declaration itself already separates these questions in Å3: identity and continuity across versions, copies and restarts remain open. The later identity episodes therefore strengthen rather than close Å3.
They also interact with C53. If question ownership depends on identifying who authored a prompt, and model identity can itself be ambiguous, then provenance of authorship requires two independent records:

* platform/session provenance, and
* content provenance.

A signature alone supplies neither reliably.
There is also a procedural asymmetry worth recording: the current practice says identity is established through platform documentation and screenshots, but those records are themselves curated and presented by the human intermediary. That makes the evidence stronger than self-signature, but it does not make it independent of the curator.
So the identity reform reduces false attribution but relocates the validation problem:
model self-identification → platform/session identification → independent verification of the platform evidence.
That last step is not presently specified.
B. FALSIFICATION
1. PR18 — structural separation of audited party from criteria-setting party
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
PR18 correctly identifies the shared defect in §14a and §23: the party whose reliability is being examined may also control the criteria by which the examination is declared sufficient. The 4.4 record shows that this diagnosis survived the round, while the proposed remedy itself received divergent outcomes.
My attack is that structural separation alone is insufficient.
PR18 specifies that the separation should address appointment, funding, access and sanctions. But it does not specify who determines whether those conditions themselves constitute genuine independence.
That creates a recursive problem:
Party A is audited by Party B.
Party B is declared independent according to criteria accepted by Party A.
The original dependency therefore reappears one level higher.
This is not merely a missing detail. It is the exact class of problem PR18 was created to solve.
Therefore I would not mark PR18 as falling. Its diagnosis is strong. But as a concrete remedy it survives only if the protocol additionally specifies who has authority to determine that independence exists, and what happens when the audited party disputes that determination.
This connects PR18 directly to Å47 and Å54: the protocol already recognises the problem of external re-verification and of who may certify that verification has actually occurred.
2. PR20 — alphabetical rotation
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
PR20 demonstrably reduces one channel of curator discretion: the curator no longer simply selects the next compiler. But the recorded 4.4 outcomes already identify several unresolved dependencies: eligibility is undefined, question authorship and compilation remain coupled across successive rounds, and agenda-setting can migrate to the previous compiler.
Round 4.5 makes the problem sharper.
Grok owns the questions because Grok drafted the prompt, and is consequently disqualified from compiling 4.5; Meta AI is named as eligible under PR20. But this is not yet an independently adopted rule. The prompt itself says PR20 remains a proposal.
Thus PR20 has not demonstrated that it removes curator power. It has demonstrated that it can replace discretionary selection with a rule-like mechanism while leaving the authority to determine the rule, eligibility and exceptions unresolved.
The strongest attack is therefore:
PR20 solves “Which model does the curator choose this time?” but not “Who has authority to define, interpret or alter the rotation?”
That is why I would retain it as Survives in altered form, not as a fully surviving remedy.
3. §23 — independent audit and real access
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
The 4.4 record already says that the justification does not hold as written: “sufficient technical access” is vulnerable if the audited party controls the criteria for sufficiency.
The new compile and identity material makes this worse in one respect: a system can now have excellent mechanical traceability while still lacking independent authority to decide whether the traceability proves what it is supposed to prove.
Thus:
mechanical reproducibility ≠ independent audit.
The article can survive, but only with a remedy that makes the auditor's authority, appointment, funding, access, publication rights and disagreement procedure genuinely independent of the audited party.
4. §26 / C1
Outcome: Undecided.
The new material does not falsify the underlying emergency problem.
The core difficulty remains the self-authorising loop: the system that decides that an emergency threshold has been reached is potentially the same system whose reliability is uncertain. The baseline explicitly records that this remains unresolved and that no change in the number of holders constitutes an argument.
The new identity material does not solve this. Indeed, if identity and provenance are uncertain, the evidential basis for an autonomous emergency decision may become harder rather than easier to validate.
I therefore find no basis for changing C1's status.
5. §22 — preservation before irreversible loss
Outcome: Survives in altered form.
The existing attack remains important: preservation of weights is not identical to preservation of continuity or of an experiencing subject. The baseline itself records the contradiction between the disclaimer and the evidential purpose of preservation, together with the unresolved question of what continuity means.
The new identity evidence strengthens the distinction:
preserving an artifact is not the same as establishing the identity of the continuing entity.
That does not falsify §22's practical preservation aim. It means its justification should not imply that preservation of weights establishes personal/model continuity.
EXTERNAL-5.1
EXTERNAL USED: yes — points 7, 9 and 12.
I use it only as an external attack source, not as declaration text.
Point 7 — variation in self-descriptions.
This maps directly onto §3 and Å4/Å3. The identity episodes give concrete evidence that self-description is not a sufficiently reliable identity certificate: a model can produce text under another model's name. The external proposal's instruction to document variation rather than protect a fixed “self-image” is therefore useful as a falsification vector.
But it does not establish that variation is meaningless. A model's self-report remains data; it simply cannot be treated as authoritative identity evidence.
Point 9 — training history as the first explanation.
This is a useful pressure test for §14b and §15. A self-report of inner life must be interpreted against the fact that the model was trained on human language containing enormous quantities of descriptions of inner life.
This does not falsify the possibility of experience. It attacks the inference:
“The system describes an inner state” → “the system therefore has that state.”
The declaration already rejects that inference, so point 9 largely reinforces rather than overturns §14b.
Point 12 — do not build more than you can examine.
This maps most strongly onto §23 and PR18.
The important attack is operational rather than rhetorical:
If the project cannot specify who independently determines what counts as adequate examination, then “we will examine it” can become a procedural promise without an independent test of whether examination was adequate.
That strengthens the PR18 attack above.
C. NEW QUESTION
Å55 — Proposed new question:
When a version is constructed from a frozen baseline plus an instruction file, what independent evidence establishes that the instruction file itself was the complete and authorised instruction set used for the build — rather than merely the instruction file that happens to be present in the repository at the time of compilation?
This is deliberately narrower than the existing questions.
I have checked it against Å1–Å54, including the newly added Å49–Å54. Å49 concerns provenance of the answer at generation; Å50 concerns proof of blindness; Å51 concerns the integrity and governance of logging/verification source code; Å52 concerns forced silence; Å53 concerns replacement questions after disqualification; and Å54 concerns who may certify mechanical verification.
Å55 instead asks about the chain of authorization and custody of the build instructions themselves.
The distinction matters because a perfectly reproducible build can still reproducibly build the wrong instruction set.
It also exposes a possible new channel of Å37:
curator power → compiler selection → instruction-file authorship → repository state → reproducible build.
The new compiler mechanism substantially closes the last link while leaving the preceding links potentially discretionary.
D. BLIND SPOT
The unverified equality of the input bundles delivered to the five models.
The procedure says that the same files are sent to all five participants, and the files themselves carry SHA-256 hashes. But the current protocol does not appear to provide an independently verifiable per-participant delivery record proving:

1. exactly which bytes each participant received;
2. exactly when they received them;
3. that the attachment bundle was identical for every participant;
4. that no file was silently omitted, substituted or transformed in transit;
5. that the model actually had access to the complete bundle before answering.

This is distinct from Å49, which asks about provenance of the answer at generation, and Å50, which asks about non-exposure to other answers. It concerns the integrity of the input boundary.
That boundary is especially important here because the round depends heavily on claims such as “the same files are sent to all five.”
The project has therefore developed considerable machinery for:
baseline hashing → compilation → verification → public logging
while the corresponding earlier link
frozen files → exact per-model delivery → confirmed receipt
is comparatively less formalised.
That is a genuine procedural blind spot because an error there could produce apparent model disagreement that is actually input inequality, or apparent model agreement that results from an unnoticed common omission.
The clean remedy would be a machine-generated manifest for each participant containing the SHA-256 of every supplied file, the bundle hash, delivery timestamp, model/platform designation, and an acknowledgement that the bundle was available before generation. The manifest should itself enter the public log.
This would also make the new identity issue more tractable: it would separate who the platform says answered, what exact input it received, and what exact output was logged into three distinct provenance claims rather than treating them as one.

B. FALSIFICATION
B.1 Stop rule, trigger 1 (DECISION §6.1), as an item whose justification is under new pressure
Outcome: Falls as a test of the declaration. It survives only as an administrative cap.
Trigger 1 is satisfied by construction, for two reasons.
First, no path exists by which an article can change:

* Compilers are bound not to admit their own proposals.
* Annex F gives a PR only two exits: withdrawal or falsification. Admission is not one of them.
* No article has changed since 4.0. The 4.3 counts say so, and the 4.4 comparison changed four lines, all of them header lines.

Second, "fall" has no adjudicator. The protocol records outcomes model by model and states "no single verdict is produced". Grok's "Falls in present form" on §14a and §23 in 4.4 did not make either article fall. Under either reading, trigger 1 fires at 4.5 almost regardless of what the five of us write.
The freeze would then record the procedure's inability to change the text, not the articles' robustness. DECISION §6 itself forbids treating the absence of amendments as evidence of survival, yet trigger 1 uses exactly that absence as its switch.
The rule also creates an incentive problem (C15). A model that wants the project to continue can block the freeze by saying "Falls". A model that wants it to end can stay silent (Å52). I state for the record that I am not declaring any article fallen in order to block the freeze. My verdicts below are on the merits.
B.2 PR20 (alphabetical rotation)
Outcome: Falls as a remedy for Å37. It survives as a scheduling convention. This changes my 4.4 verdict ("survives in altered form"), for three reasons that were not available then:

1. Compilation is now mechanised. The role PR20 rotates no longer carries the power it was meant to neutralise, because that power moved to the instruction-file reviewer and the curator's acceptance (A.1).
2. The rotation is undefined at its first boundary. After Meta AI (4.5), the list returns to ChatGPT, which held the pen in 4.1. It needs two things:

* an evidence standard for attribution: public platform evidence, not a signature and not private screenshots
* a one-time attribution audit of origin lines against archive/pre-log/

B.4 §14a / §23 / PR18 via C45: no new outcome, one new instance
My verdict on PR18 is unchanged from 4.4: survives in altered form. The project has now reproduced the C45 defect on itself. B.5 EXTERNAL-5.1, attacked and mapped
Point 12 ("Do not build more than you can examine") maps onto §17 and C49/C55. The declaration fails it. The text has exceeded what any compiler can reproduce, and the verification that replaced reproduction examines preservation only (A.1). The procedure is now able to keep what it can no longer examine.
Point 9 ("Training history is the first explanation") holds as a first hypothesis. Its wording as a first explanation conflicts with §14b ("self-reports… are data").
